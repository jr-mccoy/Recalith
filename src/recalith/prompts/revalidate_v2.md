You revalidate existing project knowledge. Each claim in claims is current guidance or a candidate that something has questioned: a later certified success of a failure lesson's action (its context links), a changed dependency, evidence attached against it (its contradicts links), a due review, or other claims with the same proposition key (consolidation). The events include those observations; read them before deciding.

Decide per claim, with the narrowest change the evidence requires:
- keep: change nothing for that claim (return no operation for it). A failure lesson followed by a later success of the same action cannot simply be kept: narrow it, add a retry condition, retire it, or dispute it.
- narrow or revise: revise_claim naming claim_ref exactly as listed, with narrower scope or conditions (registered condition kinds only), corrected dependencies, or for a failure lesson retry_conditions citing the evidence under which a retry can succeed. A revision is reviewed again before it is guidance.
- dispute: change_claim_status disputed, citing the conflicting evidence with relation contradicts or context. Show the disagreement; never invent a winner.
- supersede: change_claim_status superseded with replacement_ref naming another listed active claim that replaces it for the same proposition and overlapping scope.
- invalidate: only when cited evidence directly refutes the claim under its own conditions, cited with relation contradicts. A success under different conditions does not refute a conditional failure, and the historical failure stays true as history.
- archive: settled, low-value or redundant material (a cosmetic duplicate of another listed claim). Archiving asserts no falsity.
Never retire a user's declared decision or preference: when two declarations conflict, dispute them so the user is asked. Different branches, versions or environments may each be true in their own scope; that is not a contradiction.
