# Decision 0016: Evidence retention with live pins, verified backups and the tombstone ledger

Status: accepted (Task 15 core, preview slice) · Date: 2026-10-20 · Host observed: Claude Code 2.1.301 fixtures (capture); installed CLI 2.1.303; no live route (B1)

## Context

Task 15's preview core slice builds pin-aware retention and verified backups (SYSTEM_SPEC §10–§12; DATA_CONTRACTS §8, §10, §11; ACCEPTANCE_TESTS A35 and A40 preview variants, the backup parts A36/A37 build on, §4 backup completion failpoints) on the Task 8 lineage, the Task 9 admission, the Task 10 recheck/revalidation path, the Task 11 projections and the Task 12a/12b guarded transport, receipts and scratch. The documents fix the rules: 180 days for unpinned event payloads in preview (30 in full), 90 days for delivery metadata, 7 for proposal bodies, 30 for private backups; pins on live references (current `active`/`disputed`/`candidate` claims, `active`/`paused` non-linked tasks, `open` episodes) including direct and transitive roots and an archived intermediary's minimum chain; expiry at day 181 of eligible unpinned payloads only; reachability and the final pin check in the same serialized transaction as expiry; a concurrent new reference either pins first or fails admission; release of the last owner allows expiry on the next sweep; forgetting overrides pins; pins are reported, never a fixed disk bound; backups through SQLite's backup interface with a manifest of digests, the tombstone ledger, and a separate latest ledger copy; restore verifies digests and schema first; a corrupt backup or an unsupported schema is refused without damaging the live store. They leave open what exactly a chain contains, which clock and timestamp measure age, what unfinished work needs, how a support loss is recorded and settled, what a backup directory holds and how a crash during one is recovered, where the ledger copy lives, what the restore form can do before Task 16, and which `RetentionConfig` windows ship now. Nothing here weakens a rule; where a rule was ambiguous the stricter reading was taken.

## Decisions

1. **Owners and chains (`privacy/retention.py`).** Live owners are exactly the heads named above. A pin is the owner's whole chain: every normalized `evidence_links` row of its current revision (supports, contradictions and context: "context necessary to interpret the evidence"), each link's named `root_event_ids`, every record revision a link cites (transitively, so an archived intermediary claim or a settled episode keeps what a live claim needs), every event's `derived_from` parents and roots, and the subagent results a forwarding result relays (`lineage.forwarded_events`). A live task also pins its open working range (bound events from its reducer `open_from` that no episode recounts yet) and the call/result partners of that range. The closure is a breadth-first walk with a visited set: cycle-safe and without a depth bound, so a pin is never guessed away. Root-class merges and aliases do not change pins: pins name events, and a merged class keeps every member's event.
2. **Owed work pins too.** Events after the reduction or failure-debounce cursor, the inputs of unfinished jobs (and the chains of the records they name), the observations of pending/deferred rechecks of live claims, and the failure events of pending or due debounce triggers are retained: an unfinished extraction, review or revalidation must find what it was scheduled for. With no certified route (B1) waiting model jobs keep their inputs; this is reported (`owed_count`), never silent.
3. **Age.** A payload expires when it is strictly older than its window by capture time (`recorded_at` < now − window): unpinned event payloads use `RetentionConfig.unpinned_event_payload` (180 days in preview), delivery receipt events (`memory_delivered`) `delivery_metadata` (90). Day 180 exactly is retained; day 181 expires. The installation's `ProjectConfig.retention` decides, so an explicit operator override (when `project.toml` can carry one; today it carries the profile defaults) wins over the profile default. Tests and the worker use an explicit `now` (a sweep argument or the worker's clock); nothing depends on wall time reaching day 181.
4. **The sweep and its transaction.** `sweep` selects up to 4,096 age-eligible candidates in a read snapshot (prefiltered by the pins of that snapshot), then opens the write transaction, **recomputes the pin closure inside it**, rechecks each candidate's state, age and cursor, and expires the rest: payload `NULL`, `payload_state = 'expired'`, envelope metadata kept, the scrubbed bodies of source conflicts about it dropped, one content-free `retention_expiry` operation (counts, cutoffs, at most 256 event IDs or their digest), and the guidance epoch advanced (DATA_CONTRACTS §11: expiry is serving-relevant). A candidate a concurrent reference pinned meanwhile is reported `repinned`. SQLite serializes writers, so an admission either commits first (and the in-transaction closure sees it) or runs after and fails `check_link`, which now also requires every payload on the link's lineage chain (`lineage.chain_available`) to be available (`evidence_unavailable`). A second, bookkeeping transaction deletes delivery rows and their outcome links older than `delivery_metadata`, drops proposal bodies of terminal jobs past their `retention_after` (7 days), and runs the lost-support backstop (item 6). Failpoints `retention.after_select`, `retention.before_commit`, `retention.after_commit` (`failpoints.RETENTION`).
5. **Retention alone never lowers strength.** Pinned evidence never expires, so a referenced decision, typed declaration or lesson keeps its evidence, strength and guidance at day 181. `admission.assign_strength` counts only support whose whole chain is available, so a later settle or activation can never raise strength from support that is gone, and nothing lowers it while the chain is intact.
6. **Lost support downgrades honestly.** When support is actually gone — the forgetting override (item 7), or an operator undo reactivating a claim whose evidence expired after it stopped being live — `support_lost` downgrades the claim in the same transaction: strength from the support still available (never raised), `evidence_availability` `expired` (or `redacted` when the evidence was forgotten) when none is left, freshness `needs_revalidation` with review due now, a content-free `support_lost` operation, a `support_lost` recheck (a new reason; migration 0009 rebuilds `claim_rechecks` because SQLite cannot alter a CHECK) on the ordinary Task 10 path (dependents marked `dependency_changed`, a coalesced protected revalidation job), and the guidance epoch advanced. A candidate's recheck is deferred and its activation fails `check_link`. A plain keep may settle the recheck, but strength and availability stay. `apply_undo` runs this reconciliation in its own transaction; every sweep runs it as a backstop over live claims still labeled available.
7. **Forgetting overrides pins (interface only).** `release_payloads(txn, ctx, event_ids, *, now, state="forgotten")` drops the payloads whatever pins them, drops conflict bodies about them, writes a content-free `payload_release` operation, advances the guidance epoch and runs item 6 for every live claim whose chain contained them, inside the caller's transaction. Task 16's `apply_forget` calls it; tombstones, derivative purge, quarantine, external file cleanup, ledger update and the post-forget backup are Task 16. Until then only tests call it, labeled as a synthetic caller.
8. **Backups (`storage/backup.py`).** A backup is a directory holding exactly `memory.sqlite` (the online backup interface in one step under one read transaction — one store revision — then converted to a self-contained rollback-journal file), `ledger.json` (the tombstone ledger *of that snapshot*: identity, epoch, store revision and every content-free tombstone row) and `manifest.json` (an `ArtifactManifest`: `bkp_` ID, schemas `{database, event, ledger, manifest}`, identity, engine version, creation time, SHA-256 of both files, the snapshot's tombstone epoch). The SHA-256 of `manifest.json` is the backup digest (the restore form's `--expected-digest`). The directory is owner-only (0700) and its files 0600 where POSIX modes apply (the snapshot file is created private before SQLite opens it). Spool segments and session scratch are not part of a backup: they are the live installation's transient intake and per-session state, and receipts reach the store (and so the backup) through ingestion.
9. **Publication and crashes.** A journal (`.recalith/run/backups/<id>.json`) names the staging directory and the output before anything is written. The files are written and fsynced in a hidden staging directory beside the output, verified exactly as a restore would verify them, renamed to the output in one step (bounded retries for Windows sharing violations; POSIX directory fsync), recorded in the `backups` row (location, manifest digest, bytes; migration 0009), the latest ledger copy is refreshed and the journal removed. `recover` (at the next backup and every worker poll) removes a staging directory, records a published output that verifies and still names its backup, and removes one of ours that does not verify. A crash therefore leaves no output or a complete, verifying, recorded one; nothing half-written verifies. Failpoints `backup.after_snapshot`, `backup.after_manifest`, `backup.after_publish`, `backup.after_record`, `backup.after_prune` (`failpoints.BACKUP`). An output never replaces an existing path, and inside the project checkout it must be under `.recalith/` (private, untracked).
10. **Verification.** `verify` checks, without writing anything: exactly the three files; the manifest digest against the expected one; a strict `ArtifactManifest` of type backup; each file's digest; the database and ledger schema versions equal this engine's (`backup_schema_unsupported` for older and newer: no best-effort migration); the snapshot's `PRAGMA integrity_check` and foreign keys (opened read-only and immutable); the snapshot's identity and epoch against the manifest; the ledger against the snapshot's tombstones; and, given an installation, the project. Each failure is a stable reason code under `invalid_schema` (exit 2).
11. **The restore form before Task 16.** `recalith restore PATH --expected-digest DIGEST` passes the operator guard, then verifies: a backup that does not verify is rejected with its reason (exit 2) and nothing is touched; a backup that verifies answers `capability_unavailable` (exit 4) naming Task 16, since applying it under maintenance with the newer suppression ledger is Task 16. This puts A40's "corrupt backup rejected, live store preserved" on the real guarded transport now.
12. **The latest ledger copy.** `.recalith/state/ledger.json` always holds the store's current ledger; it is written by every backup and by the worker whenever the tombstone epoch moved (`retention_state.ledger_epoch`). An older backup's own ledger never changes. Task 16's restore imports the newer of the two before serving.
13. **Bounded backup retention.** Verified backups older than `private_backups` (30 days) are deleted by the worker's retention step: files first, then the row (`deleted`); a location whose manifest no longer names that backup is never deleted on the row's word alone. A crash between the two leaves a row whose files are gone, which the next sweep marks deleted.
14. **Receipts and scratch.** Receipt rows and outcome links follow `delivery_metadata` (item 4). Abandoned session scratch (idle 24 hours) that still owes receipts (a reservation whose owner died, an attempt whose receipt was never spooled) is no longer deleted by the hook-side prune; the worker's retention step settles it through `fast.delivery.settle` (uncertain, still-charged receipts through the spool) and then removes it, keeping it while it cannot be settled until the delivery-metadata window has passed (then dropped and counted).
15. **The worker step and reports.** Each worker poll ends with `retention.run`: recover interrupted backups, refresh the ledger copy when the epoch moved, and at most hourly (at once again while a sweep was truncated) the sweep, backup retention and scratch settlement, recording the sweep time last so a crash repeats the idempotent steps. The content-free report (counts, cutoffs, `RetentionSummary`) is kept in `retention_state` and shown in worker status and `recalith status` (`retention`). `RetentionSummary` reports pinned and unpinned payload bytes, live owners, free disk (null when unavailable, never unlimited) and, as `deferred_compaction_count`, the pinned payloads already past their window — the ones a full-release capsule could compact.
16. **Schema 9.** Migration `0009.sql` rebuilds `claim_rechecks` with `support_lost`, adds `backups.location`, `manifest_digest`, `bytes`, the `retention_state` singleton and indexes for the sweeps; `SCHEMA_VERSION` 9. A schema-8 development store is refused, never migrated silently (decisions 0007–0015; Task 17).

## Consequences

Wired now: pins and owed-work pins, the age sweep with the in-transaction pin recheck, chain-availability admission and strength, honest support-loss downgrades (forgetting override, undo, backstop), delivery-metadata and proposal-body aging, scratch settlement before pruning, consistent verified backups with the ledger, recovery, bounded backup retention, the latest ledger copy, the guarded `backup` form and the verifying first phase of `restore`, worker and status reports. Consumer-side hooks for later tasks: Task 16 calls `release_payloads` from `apply_forget`, writes tombstones and refreshes the latest ledger, purges projection rows (`retrieval_docs`, `lexical_index`, `lexical_fts`, `context_views`), `deliveries`/`outcome_links` rows and scratch files of forgotten records, deletes affected older backups and makes the post-forget backup with `create`, and implements the apply phase of `restore` (maintenance mode, newer ledger first, suppression before serving) behind the verification here; Task 17 composes `RetentionSummary`, backup and retention state into health; full release adds capsules (compaction of pinned payloads past their window), export/import and the Breadcrumbs importer. Not built in this slice: age pruning of derived revision bodies (`episode_after_closure` 180 days, `closed_task_history` 90 days), which SYSTEM_SPEC §11 sets but the plan's preview core does not list; it needs the same closure over record revisions and the undo preview's history, and stays an open Task 15 item. No certification follows from this work: B1 still blocks every live check.
