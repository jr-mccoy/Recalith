# Decision 0017: Intentional forgetting and suppression-aware restore

Status: accepted (Task 16, preview) · Date: 2026-10-20 · Host observed: Claude Code 2.1.301 fixtures (capture); installed CLI 2.1.304; no live route (B1)

## Context

Task 16 builds intentional forgetting and the apply phase of restore (SYSTEM_SPEC §11–§12; DATA_CONTRACTS §5, §8, §10–§12; ACCEPTANCE_TESTS A25, A36, A37, A48, A63, A64) on the Task 15 core: `retention.release_payloads` (the pin override), `support_lost`/`reconcile`, verified backups with their ledger, the latest ledger copy and the verifying first phase of `restore` (decision 0016), the Task 11 projections, the Task 12b delivery fence, receipts and scratch, and the Task 5 fenced application. The documents fix the rules: an operator-only, digest-guarded forget over records, tasks, source ranges or a saved exact query; maintenance mode with delivery and model launches paused; a dependency closure; one transaction that writes content-free tombstones and a new epoch, purges bodies, revisions and proposals and invalidates projections, quarantining a derivative that keeps surviving roots and forgetting one that keeps none; no model in that transaction; file cleanup, the ledger update, a fresh verified backup and the deletion of affected older backups after it (privacy first when the backup fails); reconstruction as later admitted work; recovery before delivery resumes; replay suppression without paraphrase claims; restore applying the newer ledger before serving; the limits of local deletion reported. They leave open what a selector's closure contains, what a quarantined or forgotten head looks like for tasks and episodes, how replay is recognized, how a late model result is kept out, where the post-forget backup lives, how phases persist and resume, and how a restore swaps stores safely. Where a rule was ambiguous the stricter reading was taken.

## Decisions

1. **Selectors (`privacy/forget.py`).** `record_refs` (claims, tasks, episodes or events; capsules are full-release), `task_ids` (the task, every linked placeholder redirecting to it, the events bound to them, their episodes, and the events of their sessions not bound yet — unreduced in the store or still in the spool), `source_ranges` (one adapter's host session: the adapter's source sequence, or for an adapter that reports none — Claude Code 2.1.301 — the 1-based capture ordinal in that session, spooled events after stored ones) and `saved_query` (its exact record refs). A selector naming something that is not this installation's is `invalid_schema`.
2. **The closure.** Events derived from, rooted in or relaying (`lineage.forwarded_events`) a forgotten event are forgotten; every record any revision of which cites forgotten content (an event link, a named root, a record link, a claim dependency) joins it, transitively; the `memory_delivered` receipts naming a closure record are forgotten; records already forgotten are skipped, and an earlier quarantined head is skipped unless selected. A selected record is **forgotten**; a derivative whose current revision keeps a surviving root (an available event outside the closure — for a claim through its supports, for a task or episode through any link — or a record link outside it) is **quarantined**, otherwise forgotten. The affected backups are the recorded verified backups whose snapshot still holds a closure payload or body (unreadable counts as affected); exports are listed as external copies (none exist in preview). The preview digest covers the selector, the stored closure, the head revisions, the affected backups and exports and the tombstone epoch; spooled events are transient intake and are not part of it.
3. **Heads after the purge.** Every revision body of a closure record is set to `NULL` and a body-less marker revision (`revision + 1`, status `forgotten` or `quarantined`) becomes its head; claim heads lose their content key, failure family and review time. Migration `0010.sql` (schema 10) rebuilds `task_heads` and `episode_heads` with the two head states (SQLite cannot alter a CHECK). `show` returns a content-free marker for such a head and refuses its revisions. The reducer never binds a new event to a forgotten or quarantined task: the session's next event opens a new provisional task, and a late result of a forgotten call joins the session's current task.
4. **The purge transaction.** Inside one write transaction, with `secure_delete` on: the closure is recomputed and must reproduce the preview digest (else `revision_conflict`, exit 3, nothing changed); `release_payloads` drops the payloads (pins never block it) and downgrades live claims that lost support; each forgotten event's envelope becomes a content-free shell (identities, times, channel, authority, roots, coverage; no locator, no extensions); conflict bodies, root descriptors, environment observations taken from those events, artifact-write paths, pending triggers and rechecks go; the records are purged (item 3) with their links, dependencies, held supersessions, intent-question keys, reducer bookkeeping and binding-candidate annotations; unfinished jobs whose inputs (or failure trigger) touch the closure are cancelled (`forgotten_input`); proposal bodies of those jobs, of applications that changed a closure record and of any proposal naming one are dropped; operation inputs and reasons naming the closure become `{purged, kind}` (application records, `@apply`, keep their content-free result); deliveries and outcome links naming the closure go; views are dropped (republished after maintenance), the lexical rows of each record are removed and the FTS index is merged (`optimize`) so deleted terms leave its shadow tables; the tombstone (`frg_` ID derived from the preview digest; target IDs = every forgotten event, stored or spooled, and every forgotten record; source digests = the range and saved-query digests; never the operator's reason text), its `tombstone_targets` rows, the new tombstone epoch, a content-free `forget` audit and the persisted `privacy_operations` row commit together. No model or network call runs in it. The spool and the session scratch are cleaned before the commit too (item 6), so after the commit no forgotten text remains in the store, the spool or the scratch.
5. **Replay suppression.** `repository.insert_event` stores nothing for an event whose derived ID is a tombstone target (`suppressed`): an identical or changed replay of a known source, an old spool segment or a transcript re-read never resurrects content and never records a conflict body. A new statement has a new source identity and is ordinary evidence (extraction, review, admission). The guarded typed declaration derives its event from its content, so a declaration whose event was forgotten is re-keyed by the current tombstone epoch: the same statement typed again is a new statement, while a crash retry inside one epoch stays idempotent. The hook's post-tool overlay skips an event the latest ledger copy names, and skips overlays entirely while maintenance is active. Paraphrases are not detected (that would need the forgotten content).
6. **Maintenance, phases and recovery.** An exclusive privacy lock (`.recalith/run/privacy.lock`) serializes forget, restore and their recovery; the maintenance marker (`.recalith/run/maintenance.json`) pauses delivery (the hook checks it before compiling and again under the session lock: `maintenance_in_progress`), the post-tool overlay and model launches (the lane leases nothing). Phases persist in `privacy_operations` (`purged → files → ledger → backup → deleted → reconstructed → completed`): file cleanup (ready spool segments rewritten atomically without the targets, or removed; quarantined or temporary spool files and stale scratch temporaries naming a target removed; every session scratch rewritten under its session lock without forgotten outcomes, items and restoration keys, totals and attempts kept; a scratch whose lock is busy stays owed and the operation reports `cleanup_pending` with `scratch_cleanup_pending`), the latest ledger copy, the post-forget backup, the deletion of affected backups, the reconstruction jobs. The marker is removed only when no operation is unfinished and no restore is journaled. A crash resumes: the worker's poll (`forget.resume`, before any other step, never blocking an operator command that holds the lock), the next forget (idempotent by preview digest: the same request returns the persisted operation's result) or a restore. A marker whose lock is free and with nothing unfinished is stale and is removed.
7. **Late results.** A cancelled job's lease is fenced, so its late proposal is never persisted. A proposal whose read set carries an older tombstone epoch is persisted without its body and re-evaluated in the same transaction (`tombstone_epoch_changed`): a response prepared before a forget, citing the forgotten source or not, never stores what it restated and never applies.
8. **The post-forget backup and older backups.** After the ledger, `backup.create` writes a fresh verified backup to `.recalith/backups/forget-<id>` (deterministic, so a resumed phase finds and records it); its snapshot holds the new epoch, the tombstone and the quarantined heads. Then every affected older backup is deleted (files, then the row), also when the new backup failed: the result is `backup_state: unavailable` with `backup_unavailable_after_forget`, the purge and the ledger stay, and nothing is rolled back. Unaffected backups remain.
9. **Reconstruction.** For each quarantined claim with surviving event roots an `extract_claims` job over those roots is queued after the commit (idempotent per claim and epoch). Its proposal is ordinary: a weak candidate, its own review, admission. Without a certified backend (B1) it waits visibly; the quarantined placeholder is never served and stays quarantined (a reconstructed claim is a new claim). Quarantined tasks and episodes are not rebuilt in preview: they stay content-free and their sessions continue in new tasks.
10. **Restore** (`forget.restore`, behind the Task 15 verification). The backup must name this installation (`backup_foreign_installation`). Under the privacy lock the worker is asked to stop and the installation lock is taken (else `capability_unavailable`, nothing changed); an unfinished forget on the live store finishes first; every tombstone known anywhere is merged (the backup's own ledger, the live store's, the latest ledger copy); the marker pauses delivery. The verified snapshot bytes are read once, hashed against the manifest and deserialized into memory; there, in one transaction, the missing tombstones and their targets are inserted, the same closure and purge run over their targets, revisions and epochs continue past the live store's (so no fence or cache can match old state), the live store's backup records and privacy operations replace the snapshot's, projections are reset, reconstruction is queued and a content-free `restore` audit is written. Only then is the suppressed store written to a staged file (`state/restore-<id>.sqlite`, rollback journal), journaled (`.recalith/run/restore.json`) and swapped over the live store with one atomic replace after the live WAL is checkpointed and verified empty (no sidecar can be applied to the replacement). After the swap the files are cleaned, the latest ledger is written and recorded backups older than the tombstones that still hold their content are deleted. Recovery (`recover_restore`, at the worker's start and poll before the store is opened) discards a staging restore and finishes a staged or swapped one. When the live store file is gone the guarded CLI resolves the installation from the checkout's own identity and the backup is verified against it (`identity.resolve_for_restore`). `RestoreResult` adds `source_contains_suppressed`, `deleted_backup_ids` and limit codes (`external_copy_holds_suppressed_content` when the restored source is an unrecorded copy).
11. **Limits (reported in `ForgetPreview.limits`, the result's coverage and `RestoreResult.limits`).** Explicit external exports, operator copies of backups and remote provider copies cannot be recalled by local deletion; logical forgetting is not secure erasure: filesystem forensics, a WAL not yet checkpointed (forgetting checkpoints after its phases) and third-party backups are outside it; the ledger's identifiers and digests permit membership guesses and the ledger is private; adapters' source event keys stay as opaque origin identifiers (an agent-chosen `observe` key is the caller's own text); paraphrases are not detected; an in-flight emission after the final fence cannot be recalled (A25).
12. **The fence (A25).** A forget committed before the delivery fence moves the tombstone epoch, so the reservation is withdrawn (`fenced_out`) and nothing is emitted; one committed after the fence is recorded as `in_flight_change` on the receipt and its delivery row, never claimed retracted.
13. **Schema 10.** Migration `0010.sql`: `task_heads`/`episode_heads` with `quarantined`/`forgotten`, `tombstone_targets`, an index on `privacy_operations(status)`. Schema-9 stores and backups are refused, never migrated silently (Task 17).

## Consequences

Wired now: preview and apply through the service and the guarded CLI (`forget --selector FILE --preview|--apply --preview-digest D`), the closure, the purge transaction, replay suppression, the persisted phases with recovery in the worker, the paused delivery and model lane, the post-forget backup and the deletion of affected backups, reconstruction jobs, restore (`recalith restore PATH --expected-digest D`) with the newer ledger applied in memory before anything is written or served, recovery of an interrupted restore. Left to Task 17: health composition of pending privacy maintenance, unfinished operations, `backup_unavailable_after_forget`, scratch cleanup owed and the limits; schema migration. Left to Task 13: certifying that the paused delivery holds on the real host. Full release: export/import tombstones and capsules (A38, A39, A42, A55, A71). Still open from Task 15: age pruning of derived revision bodies (`episode_after_closure`, `closed_task_history`): it needs its own pin rules for records (a settled episode cited by a live claim) and an undo story, not just the body purge. No certification follows from this work: B1 still blocks every live check.
