# Decision 0021: A bounded, worker-published lexical lookup for hook prompt context

Status: accepted (Task 11 follow-up; decision 0020 item 15) · Date: 2026-10-21 · Host observed: Claude Code CLI 2.1.306 installed (request capture only); no live route (B1)

## Context

Decision 0020 item 15 recorded a Task 11 defect the §7 gate exposed. The worker publishes one query-independent view per workspace, task and purpose (`retrieval/compile.py: _view_candidates`): the task's claims, at most 64 action-family claims, 8 disputed claims and the 32 strongest active claims, with a prompt view capped at 48 items; the 128 fallback rows are the same kind of static slice. The stdlib hook keeps only the lexically relevant candidates of that slice. A prompt-specific fact outside it never reaches the hook, while the service compiler, which searches the whole lexical index, delivers it. With one relevant fact among N near-identical heads, the hook emitted it at N = 0 and 50 and missed it at N = 200 and 10,000, so every warm and cold prompt trial of the §7 gate was degraded (about 50 % healthy delivery on every native platform against ≥ 99 % warm and ≥ 95 % cold).

SYSTEM_SPEC §5.1.1 allows the hook pre-serialized candidate packets and at most 32 already-rendered fallback rows, and forbids the full search/ranking import, Pydantic, any database write, any lock wait and any model call. The fix needs a query-dependent input that the worker prepares and the hook can read with the standard library inside the one 250 ms deadline, without weakening a filter or a fence.

## Decisions

1. **A per-workspace lookup projection (schema 11).** Migration `0011.sql` adds two disposable tables and three `projection_state` columns:
   - `lookup_candidates`: one row per (workspace, live claim head), holding the claim revision, the digest of the workspace environment it was rendered with, the guidance/policy/tombstone epochs of its read snapshot, a `volatile` flag, the IDs it cites, the payload states of the events it quotes and, when the claim is servable in that workspace, the pre-rendered candidate in exactly the primitive form views carry (`search.claim_candidate`). The one exception is bound tasks: they are stored scope-only, because bindings can change without a new claim revision and the hook re-reads them live. An unservable head (expired or redacted support, another workspace's scope) keeps a row with a null body, so the reconciler knows it was decided at that revision.
   - `lookup_postings`: (workspace, term, claim, static score) for every term of a servable row's candidate. The terms are the candidate's own `terms` (the first 64 of `index.document(...).words`, produced by `fast.context.terms`), so the indexer's and the hook's tokenizer are one function, and a posting can only point at a candidate whose relevance test can match that term.
   - `projection_state.lookup_guidance_epoch`, `lookup_policy_revision`, `lookup_tombstone_epoch`: the epochs at which the projection was last complete.
2. **The worker reconciles it, bounded, outside the write lock.** `retrieval.lookup.refresh` runs in the projection step after the index and the views, whenever its recorded epochs differ from the store's. In one read snapshot it compares every live claim head with every row and finds what is owed: a missing row; a row at another revision, environment digest or policy revision; a row whose claim is no longer live (deleted); and a volatile row rendered at an older guidance epoch. A row is volatile when its content can change without a new claim revision: a disputed claim (its text quotes evidence), non-current freshness (pending recheck text), claim dependencies (their values), or a claim that was not servable when rendered (its support may come back). It renders at most 512 owed claims in that snapshot (claim IDs in order) and then writes them in one short transaction that first re-reads the epochs: if any moved since the read, the batch is discarded and retried by the next poll, so a forget, expiry or correction committed in between can never be overwritten by older content. At most 4,096 stale rows are deleted per transaction. The epochs are recorded only when nothing remains owed. Nothing is rendered when nothing changed, so an idle worker does no lookup work. Writes never advance the store revision or the guidance epoch.
3. **Forgetting, restore and aging cooperate.** The forget purge deletes, in its own transaction and with `secure_delete`, every lookup row and posting of a forgotten or quarantined record and every row citing a purged event or record, and resets the recorded epochs. Restore's projection reset empties both tables. Aging of task and episode bodies does not touch them, since rows hold no task or episode text. The reconciler then re-renders what is still servable.
4. **The hook reads it with the standard library, within stated caps.** `fast.lookup` (new; standard library and `fast.context` only) runs for a `prompt` compile with query terms, on the view path and the fallback path alike, and only when at least 60 ms of the deadline remain (else `deadline_lookup_skipped`). In the hook's existing read-only snapshot:
   - **Terms per prompt:** the first 32 distinct terms of `fast.context.terms(text)`. Each gets a bounded document frequency (postings counted up to 1,025). The 8 rarest present terms are kept, ties broken by prompt order.
   - **Postings per term:** at most 64, strongest static score first, then claim ID.
   - **Candidate rows:** at most 16. Claims are scored by the summed term weight `1 + ln(1025 / df)`, and IDs the view or fallback rows already hold are skipped. The read is at most 32 count probes, 8 × 64 postings and 16 rows.
   Each row must pass every filter a fallback row passes, plus four stricter ones:
   - the row's policy revision equals the store's, else the lookup is refused for this compile (`lookup_policy_changed`);
   - no tombstone newer than the row's tombstone epoch names the claim or any ID it cites;
   - the claim head is still at the row's revision, status and freshness, and is live;
   - some support event still holds available or capsule-only evidence;
   - every quoted event is still in the payload state it was rendered from;
   - the environment digest equals that of the environment the compile uses;
   - a version condition whose key is known must carry a precomputed result for exactly the current value, never a guess;
   - the bound tasks are re-read live from `task_binding_heads`, so task relevance matches the service.
   The surviving candidates join the view or fallback candidates and go through the same `fast.context.decide`, ranking, budget, validation and fresh final fence (`fast.fence` checks every served head again). The hook never writes, never waits and imports nothing beyond the standard library and `recalith.fast`.
5. **Both compilers decide eligibility the same way.** `search.servable_candidate` is the single eligibility gate for pre-rendering: live head, sufficient evidence, the workspace's scope. Views, fallback rows, lookup rows and the service's `gather` all use it, and `fast.context.decide` remains the stdlib twin of `search.decide`. The service compiler keeps its unbounded lexical search, so it may find more candidates than the hook's caps admit. It never finds a different eligibility for the same candidate. A test compares them at 10,000 heads.
6. **Pre-action, session start and restoration are unchanged.** Their views keep the action-family, disputed and task slices, and they have no free-text query to look up. Correctly quiet prompts stay quiet: the lookup adds only candidates that `fast.context.relevant` accepts.
7. **Failpoints.** `lookup.before_commit` and `lookup.after_publish` join `failpoints.RETRIEVAL`, armed only programmatically, and the projection kill/restart matrix judges the lookup rows and postings with the views.
8. **Contract changes.** SYSTEM_SPEC §5.1.1 names the lookup and its caps. DATA_CONTRACTS §2.3 and §10 give the hook's added reads, the new tables and the new degradation codes `deadline_lookup_skipped` and `lookup_policy_changed`. IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN Task 11 lists the files. `SCHEMA_VERSION` is 11. As with schemas 7–10, schema-10 stores and backups are refused, never migrated silently, because upgrades are Task 17's full-only work. No target, denominator, fixture or gold label changes.

## Consequences

The hook's prompt context now reaches every servable claim through its rarest query terms, at corpus scale, on warm and cold paths, without new imports, writes or waits. Its added cost is bounded by the caps above and measured per platform by `tools/measure_release_latency.py`. The worker pays about a quarter of a millisecond per rendered claim (10,000 heads ≈ 2–3 s once, spread over bounded batches), and idles at no added cost.

Limits:
- A claim whose only matching terms are all among the 64 strongest postings of very common terms can still be crowded out. That is the cost of bounded reads. The service compiler is unaffected.
- Pending-recheck text on a non-current claim is refreshed at the next guidance epoch, as views are.
- Action-family cautions at corpus scale still come from the 64-claim action slice of the views, because pre-action lookups by family are not part of this change.
